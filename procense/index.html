Overview

We replaced paper batch records with one guided tablet flow.

Procense’s integrated platform helps chemical manufacturers run and monitor production, but every batch was still recorded on paper. I designed the Digital Forms application to change that, moving quality checks and manufacturing steps onto the tablets operators already carry.

Batches now run 10% faster, every record is traceable and accurate, and operators spend their time on the batch instead of the paperwork.

The problem

Every batch was recorded by hand, then checked on paper.

In many chemical plants, each batch is recorded on two paper forms: one for the manufacturing steps and one for the quality checks. Once complete, the forms go to directors and engineers, who decide whether the batch meets the standard. The process is slow, invites human error and holds up decisions on the floor.

Our North Star

Streamline batch manufacturing by digitizing its forms, while keeping them intuitive for operators used to filling them in by hand.

The solution, in short

A guided tablet flow, built into the Procense platform. It gives operators a faster, more reliable way to record each batch, and still feels as familiar as paper.

Approach

I started by taking the paper forms apart.

The forms captured a great deal of data, but made it hard to spot what mattered and slow to fill in.

Then I learned how the forms are used on the floor.

I interviewed Procense’s co-founder, a former process engineer who has both written and filled in these forms. Four insights stood out, and each one set a design goal.

Design goals

Four things I learned became four goals.

Each insight from the floor set one goal for the Digital Forms application, so every design decision traced back to how operators really work.

Ideation

I explored how crowded tables could become simple steps.

I studied how other products turn paper forms into software and organise large tables, then sketched layouts and noted what each form needed.

Tablet-first

We designed for desktop. The floor ran on tablets.

What we assumed

Operators would use desktops or laptops, like the rest of Procense’s web-first platform.

What we found

Operators carry tablets as they work through each step and run quality tests on the floor.

That discovery reshaped the project. We moved to a tablet-first design with new layouts and a new flow. The smaller screen meant breaking long forms into steps, which adds a tap or two, but in return operators got larger text, cleaner sections and a flow that matches how they actually work.

The solution

Two paper forms became one guided flow.

The final design merges both forms into one guided flow of four steps: batch information, special precautions, the manufacturing steps, then the quality tests. Each screen shows only what that step needs, so operators can keep their attention on the batch.

Portrait

It works however the tablet is held.

Operators flip tablets between landscape and portrait as they move around the line, so every screen reflows. Navigation tucks behind a menu, the stepper names only the current step, and fields wrap into fewer columns without losing any controls.

User feedback

A QA manager’s review cut even more typing.

We reviewed the first design of the quality test form with a QA manager, section by section, and their feedback led to two changes.

What we heard

Operators still typed too much, and choosing batch and order numbers from a list added unnecessary steps.

What we changed

More fields now fill in automatically from the sample type, and batch and order numbers are typed in directly.

Impact & reflection

Operators now record each batch as they work.

Moving from paper to the Digital Forms application streamlined the whole batch process. Operators record data as they go instead of copying it later, reviewers see it as soon as it’s entered, and every value carries who entered it and when. With the paperwork out of the way, teams can focus on running safe, consistent batches.

What I learned

Design for the device in their hands

Learning that operators carry tablets changed every layout decision, and made the forms easier to use, not harder.

Modernize what people already know

Keeping the forms familiar, rather than reinventing them, let the plants move faster without losing the checks that keep every batch safe.
